Wood fencing

Wood fence installation in Fargo, ND

Wood privacy, picket, and horizontal fences for people who want to stain the line to the house. The part that fails first in Fargo is almost always the post, not the board.

When this fence is the right job

Wood is the right job when you want a screen you can paint or stain, or a horizontal look the vinyl catalog does not match. It is the wrong job if nobody will seal it. Bare wood in this climate cups, grays, and rots at the bottom where snow sits.

How the work goes

  1. 1Choose the build. Solid privacy, board-on-board, horizontal, or picket. The style changes the lumber list and the wind load.
  2. 2Mark the yards. In Fargo the front yard stays short and mostly open. The taller wood line starts at the side, past the front of the house.
  3. 3Set posts in concrete. Holes go deep enough for this clay. The concrete is crowned so meltwater does not pool at the post.
  4. 4Hang rails and boards. Boards go on after the posts have a set. A gate is framed as its own opening, with posts that can carry the weight.
  5. 5Leave a finish plan. We can talk through stain, but the fence still needs a finish after it is up if you want the wood to last.

Horizontal wood looks clean and it also acts like a sail. On an open lot south of town or west of West Fargo, post spacing gets tighter than it would on a sheltered city lot. If the posts from the last fence have already jacked out of the ground, new boards on those posts will lean again by spring.

What changes the estimate

We price the line on the lot. These are the pieces that move the number. We do not publish a per-foot menu.

Style

Privacy, shadowbox, horizontal, and picket use different amounts of lumber.

Height

A 6-foot privacy line uses more board and takes more wind than a 4-foot picket.

Post depth

Deep holes in lake clay are a real share of the labor on a wood fence here.

Gates

A wood gate is heavy. The posts and hardware are priced with the opening.

Finish

The quote covers the fence. Stain is a separate decision, and unfinished wood will not stay new.

Tear-out

Old posts with concrete bells are slow to pull, especially when the ground is still wet in spring.

Questions about wood fencing

Which wood holds up in Fargo?

Treated pine is the usual board and post. Cedar is an option when the look matters more than the lowest material price. Either one needs the cut ends and the bottom of the boards kept out of standing meltwater. The post footing decides whether the fence is still straight in three years.

Do I have to stain a wood fence?

If you want it to last and to keep a color, yes. Sun, wind, and snow bleach bare wood fast on the prairie. We do not sell a wood fence as a no-maintenance product. If that upkeep is the thing you want to avoid, vinyl is the closer match.

Will the posts heave out of the ground?

They will if they are set shallow in this clay. Fargo sits on lake-plain soil that holds water, and frost goes several feet down. Published footing notes for structures in North Dakota run to about 60 inches. A fence post is not a building footing, but a post set at a southern depth will move. We set them deep and crown the concrete.

Is horizontal wood a problem in the wind?

It can be. A solid horizontal run catches more wind than a picket, and an open lot has nothing to block a west wind. We tighten post spacing on those runs. A sheltered backyard in town is a milder case than a corner lot with a field behind it.

Can you replace the boards and leave the old posts?

Only when the posts are still plumb and solid. If they have jacked, split, or rotted at the ground line, new boards will lean with them. We will say which one you have after we see the line. A lot of winter calls are posts, not boards.

Talk through the line

Call or send the town, the fence type, and a rough length. We will tell you if the job is a repair, a replacement, or a different material than you started with.
