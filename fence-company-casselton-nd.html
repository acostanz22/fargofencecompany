What changes the fence here

In town, people want a yard fence and a gate. On the edge, they want wire or rail that holds animals and a gate a truck can use. Wind across open fields is part of both quotes.

Ground, rules, and the line

Casselton is not inside the City of Fargo, so Fargo’s 3-foot front-yard rule does not apply. We check the City of Casselton before we set a height, rather than importing a Fargo number. Corner visibility and the right-of-way still matter on a town lot.

Farm lines need braced corners, a post that can take wet clay, and an opening sized to the equipment. Horses and cattle do not get the same fence. On agricultural land, North Dakota’s partition-fence statute can split maintenance of a boundary fence between neighbors. A house lot is a different conversation.

Winter wind and snow drift hit an open run harder than a Fargo backyard. A solid privacy fence used as a windbreak has to be posted for that load. A wire fence lets the wind through and does not do the same job.

Questions about fencing in Casselton

Do you drive to Casselton for a yard fence?

Yes. It is inside the area we cover west of Fargo. Tell us whether the job is a house lot or a field. The material and the gate change with that answer, and the quote includes the trip only in the sense that we schedule it with other work out this way when we can.

What fence belongs on a farmstead here?

Rail or pipe if horses need to see it. Woven wire or a braced barbed line for cattle. A vinyl privacy panel belongs around the house yard, not around the pasture. Equipment gates are wide and get their own posts.

Does Fargo’s fence permit rule apply?

No. Casselton and Cass County are outside Fargo’s land-development code. Ask the city what it requires for your lot. We will build to that height. We will not guess it from a Fargo handout.
