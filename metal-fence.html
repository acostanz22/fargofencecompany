Metal fencing

Metal fence installation in Fargo, ND

Metal fencing here means steel and ornamental iron-style fences, not aluminum and not chain link. It is the heavier open fence, used where the line or the gate has to carry more load.

When this fence is the right job

Use steel when aluminum feels too light for the gate or the run, or when the look is a heavier ornamental picket. Do not use it as a substitute word for every metal fence. Chain link and aluminum are separate products with separate pages, because they fail in different ways.

How the work goes

  1. 1Separate the metal. We confirm steel versus aluminum before anyone prices the job. They do not share posts, coatings, or weight.
  2. 2Spec the coating. Bare steel rusts in this climate. The quote names a coated or painted system, not raw metal in the snow.
  3. 3Set posts for the weight. A steel panel and a drive gate need posts and footings sized for that load and for frost heave.
  4. 4Hang sections and hardware. Pickets, rails, and hinges go on after the posts are set. A gate is not hung on a line post.
  5. 5Check the bottom and the latch. Meltwater and road salt sit at the bottom rail. We look at drainage and at whether the latch still meets after the first swing.

Ornamental steel looks like aluminum from the street and behaves differently once it is in the ground. It is heavier, it can rust where the coating is cut, and it is a bad idea in a front yard if the pickets are packed tight enough to violate Fargo’s open-fence rule. We count the openness before we order a 4-foot front line.

What changes the estimate

We price the line on the lot. These are the pieces that move the number. We do not publish a per-foot menu.

Steel versus aluminum

Steel costs more to set because it weighs more. Do not compare it to an aluminum price.

Coating

A factory finish holds up better than a field paint that chips at every cut.

Gate weight

The heavier the leaf, the larger the posts and the hinges.

Openness

A packed ornamental pattern may not qualify as 75 percent open in a Fargo front yard.

Salt and meltwater

The bottom of the fence sees the most corrosion. Drainage at the post is part of the job.

Tear-out

Old steel posts in concrete are slow to remove from clay.

Questions about metal fencing

Is metal fencing the same thing as aluminum?

No. Aluminum is a light, rust-resistant ornamental fence. Steel and iron-style metal are heavier and will rust where the coating fails. Chain link is a third product. If you are comparing prices, name which one you mean or the numbers will not match.

Will a steel fence rust in Fargo?

Bare steel will. A coated system holds up if the cuts and the hinges are protected and the bottom rail is not sitting in meltwater all spring. Salt from the street speeds that up on a corner lot. We spec the coating in the quote so it is not a surprise after the first winter.

Can ornamental metal go in a Fargo front yard?

An open picket can, up to 4 feet, when it stays at least 75 percent light-permeable. A solid or tightly spaced pattern is treated more like a wall and stops at 3 feet. Corner lots also have a sight triangle. We check the pattern against that rule before it is ordered.

Can you hang a drive gate on a metal fence?

Yes, and that is one reason people step up from aluminum. The gate posts have to be sized for the leaf and set deep enough that frost does not drop the latch out of line. A drive gate is priced as its own piece, not as two extra panels.

What changes the price of a metal fence?

The metal itself, the coating, the height, the gate, and the holes in clay. A short ornamental front run and a long steel side yard are not the same quote. We measure before we price.
