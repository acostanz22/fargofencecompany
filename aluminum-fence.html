Aluminum fence installation in Fargo, ND

Aluminum is the open metal fence: pickets, rails, and a finish that does not need paint. It fits front yards, pool barriers, and any line where you want the yard visible.

When this fence is the right job

Choose aluminum when the fence has to stay open. Fargo’s front-yard rules cap a solid fence at 3 feet and allow an open fence, at least 75 percent light-permeable, up to 4 feet. A 4-foot aluminum picket usually meets that test. It is a poor choice when the job is a solid screen.

How the work goes

  1. 1Confirm the height. Front yards, street-side yards, and pool areas do not share one number. We match the style to the yard before the material is ordered.
  2. 2Lay out the posts. Flat Red River Valley lots are simple until a ditch, a walk, or a corner sight triangle shows up.
  3. 3Set posts below the frost. Posts go in concrete, deep enough for clay that heaves, with the mix crowned so meltwater does not sit on the post.
  4. 4Hang panels and gates. Sections rack where a swale drops. Gates are hung on posts sized for the leaf, not on a line post.
  5. 5Check the latch and the grade. We look for a gate that drags and for a gap a pet can use at a low spot.

Aluminum does not rust the way bare steel does, which matters when a Fargo winter throws salt and meltwater at the bottom rail. It will not hide a neighbor. If the job is privacy, look at wood or vinyl instead.

What changes the estimate

We price the line on the lot. These are the pieces that move the number. We do not publish a per-foot menu.

Height and style

A 4-foot open front-yard line and a taller pool or side-yard line are different orders.

Color

Black is the common finish. A special color waits on the manufacturer and can change the schedule.

Grade at ditches

Most Fargo yards are flat. A boulevard ditch or a low corner still needs a racked section.

Gates

A walk gate is one price. A drive opening needs heavier posts and hardware.

Digging in clay

Deep holes in wet lake clay take longer than a sandy lot, and that labor is part of the quote.

Tear-out

Pulling an old fence, including concrete around old posts, is separate from the new line.

Questions about aluminum fencing

Will an aluminum fence rust through a Fargo winter?

The aluminum itself does not rust. Salt from a sidewalk and standing meltwater still sit on the bottom rail, so the finish and the post footing matter more than the picket. We crown the concrete so water sheds off the post. If you want a solid screen, aluminum is the wrong product.

Can aluminum be used as a pool fence in Fargo?

Often, yes, because a pool barrier is supposed to be hard to climb and easy to see through. The height, the latch, and the gap under the bottom rail have to meet the rule that applies to that pool, which is not the same as a front-yard fence. We measure those gaps on the lot before the sections are ordered.

Does a 4-foot aluminum fence work in a Fargo front yard?

In the city’s residential districts, a front-yard fence is limited to 3 feet unless it is at least 75 percent light-permeable, in which case it may go to 4 feet. A standard ornamental aluminum picket is usually open enough. A corner lot also has a sight triangle. Confirm the zoning on the parcel before we order a height.

Can aluminum follow a ditch or a low corner?

Yes. Sections can rack with a mild grade. A sharp drop at a culvert is stepped or stopped short of the ditch so the bottom rail is not buried in spring runoff. Most in-town Fargo yards do not need that. The lots that do are the ones next to a swale.

What changes the price of an aluminum fence?

Length, height, gate count, and whether an old fence comes out. Digging deep post holes in wet clay, and a backyard the truck cannot reach, add labor. We price the line we measure. There is no per-foot menu on this site.
