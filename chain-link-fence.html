Chain-link fencing

Chain-link fence installation in Fargo, ND

Chain-link fences for yards, dogs, and work lots. Galvanized or coated, with or without a screen, set on posts that can take a Fargo winter.

When this fence is the right job

Chain link is the right fence when you need containment or a long run more than a finished look. It is a common dog fence and a common commercial edge. It is a weak choice when the goal is a solid, quiet screen, unless you add slats and the city still allows that height.

How the work goes

  1. 1Pick the fabric. Galvanized is the plain version. Black or green vinyl-coated fabric is the one people choose next to a house.
  2. 2Decide on a screen. Slats or a windscreen add privacy and they add wind load. In a Fargo front yard they can also change the height you are allowed.
  3. 3Set terminal posts. Ends, corners, and gates get the heavier posts, in concrete, deep enough for the clay.
  4. 4Stretch the fabric. The mesh is stretched between terminals and tied to the line posts. A loose fabric sags by the next season.
  5. 5Hang the gate. Walk gates and drive gates are framed, not a piece of mesh on light hinges.

A front-yard chain-link fence without slats is usually open enough for Fargo’s 4-foot allowance. Add privacy slats and it may no longer be 75 percent light-permeable, which pulls the limit back to 3 feet. We make that call before the slats are ordered, not after the fence is up.

What changes the estimate

We price the line on the lot. These are the pieces that move the number. We do not publish a per-foot menu.

Height and gauge

A 4-foot yard fence and a taller, heavier commercial fabric are different orders.

Coating

Coated wire costs more than plain galvanized and shows less against a house.

Slats or screen

They add material, labor, and wind load, and they can change the front-yard height rule.

Gates

A drive gate in chain link needs a frame and posts that will not twist.

Line length

Chain link is often the long fence. Corners and gates cost more than the mesh between them.

Digging

Terminal posts in wet clay take the time. Line posts follow.

Questions about chain-link fencing

Is chain link allowed in a Fargo front yard?

Yes, when it stays open. The city allows up to 4 feet in a front yard if the fence is at least 75 percent light-permeable. Plain chain link usually qualifies. A corner lot still has to keep the sight triangle clear. A solid screen on that same fence is a different rule.

Should I get galvanized or black coated chain link?

Galvanized is the lower-cost mesh and it weathers to gray. Coated fabric, often black, costs more and is what most people want beside a house. The posts and the fittings should be coated to match, or you will see bright hardware against a black fence.

Can I add privacy slats?

Yes, on the side and rear, if you also account for the wind. In a Fargo front yard, slats can drop the fence out of the 75 percent open category and pull the height limit back to 3 feet. If privacy is the real goal, a vinyl or wood screen on the side and rear is often the cleaner answer.

Will chain link hold a dog?

A 4-foot fence holds many dogs and not the ones that climb or jump. Tell us the dog. Height, a tighter mesh at the bottom, and a gate that actually latches matter more than the brand of wire. A digger needs the bottom addressed, not just a taller top.

What changes the price of a chain-link fence?

Height, gauge, coating, slats, and gates. A residential yard and a commercial lot do not use the same fabric. Pulling an old fence and digging terminals in clay are part of the labor. We measure before we quote.
